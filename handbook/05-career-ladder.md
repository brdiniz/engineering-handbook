# 05. Career ladder

**Purpose:** give every engineer and manager a clear picture of what is expected at each level, how to grow, and what evidence supports a promotion.

**Review with HR and legal before using it in a formal performance or compensation process.**

## 1. Principles

- **Levels describe scope and impact, not tenure or title.** Years of experience do not earn a level.
- **Two tracks, equal in standing.** Individual contributors and managers are different jobs, not a ladder where management is the only way up.
- **Promotion recognises sustained performance at the next level**, not potential. The usual evidence window is six months or more.
- **Expectations are cumulative.** Each level includes the expectations of the levels before it.
- **Behaviours matter as much as output.** Collaboration and judgement are assessed alongside technical delivery.

## 2. The levels

### Individual contributor track

| Level | Typical title | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| E1 | Associate Engineer | Well-defined tasks within a feature, with guidance |
| E2 | Engineer | Features within a service or area, mostly independent |
| E3 | Senior Engineer | Complex projects across components, owns outcomes and mentors others |
| E4 | Staff Engineer | Multi-team technical direction, solves ambiguous organisational problems |
| E5 | Principal Engineer | Company-wide technical strategy, shapes how the company builds |

### Management track

| Level | Typical title | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| M1 | Engineering Manager | One team, people and delivery |
| M2 | Senior Engineering Manager | Several teams or a product area |
| M3 | Director of Engineering | An engineering function, managing managers |

A **Tech Lead** is a role that a Senior or Staff Engineer takes on, not a level. See [07 Tech lead role](07-tech-lead-role.md).

## 3. Competency dimensions

Each level is assessed against five dimensions.

1. **Technical craft:** quality of design, code and technical judgement
2. **Delivery:** planning, execution and reliable outcomes
3. **Quality and operations:** testing, reliability, security and ownership in production
4. **Collaboration and communication:** working with peers, product and other teams; writing and presenting
5. **Leadership and impact:** mentoring, raising the bar, influence beyond one's own work

## 4. Expectations by level (IC track)

| Dimension | E1 | E2 | E3 | E4 | E5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Technical craft | Writes clear, tested code for well-defined tasks; learns the stack | Designs and builds features end to end in familiar areas | Designs complex systems, weighs trade-offs, picks the right level of simplicity | Sets technical direction across teams; resolves hard ambiguous problems | Defines company-wide technical strategy; recognised expert |
| Delivery | Completes assigned tasks on time, asks for help early | Delivers features reliably, breaks work into slices | Leads projects of several weeks to months, manages risks and dependencies | Drives multi-team initiatives over quarters | Drives company-level outcomes over years |
| Quality and operations | Follows team standards, fixes own bugs | Owns the quality of their features, participates in on-call | Improves reliability and testing practices of a component; leads incident response | Raises reliability and security standards across teams | Shapes the engineering approach to risk and resilience |
| Collaboration | Communicates progress clearly, accepts feedback well | Works well with product and peers, gives useful reviews | Aligns product, design and engineering; resolves disagreements constructively | Builds trust across teams, communicates direction to leadership | Represents engineering externally and influences executives |
| Leadership and impact | Supports teammates | Helps onboard new joiners | Mentors engineers; improves team practices | Develops senior engineers; changes how several teams work | Grows technical leaders; sets the culture of craft |

## 5. Expectations by level (management track)

| Dimension | M1 | M2 | M3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| People | Hires, coaches and manages performance for a team; runs effective 1:1s | Develops managers and senior ICs; handles complex people situations | Builds and develops a leadership team; shapes organisation design |
| Delivery | Team plans and ships predictably; manages scope and risk | Coordinates several teams; resolves cross-team dependencies | Owns outcomes for a function; aligns roadmap with business strategy |
| Technical judgement | Understands the team's systems well enough to challenge and unblock | Holds teams to a consistent standard of technical quality | Sets technical and operational standards for the function |
| Communication | Clear updates to leadership and stakeholders | Influences peers and senior leaders | Represents engineering to the executive team and board |
| Culture | Creates a safe, high-trust, high-standards team | Spreads good practices across teams | Defines and models engineering culture |

## 6. Promotion process

1. **Conversation.** The employee and manager discuss readiness at least twice a year.
2. **Evidence.** The manager and employee collect examples that show sustained performance at the next level across all five dimensions. Use specific projects, outcomes and feedback, not impressions.
3. **Calibration.** Managers review cases together for consistency across teams.
4. **Decision.** A promotion committee or the head of engineering decides, with written reasoning.
5. **Feedback.** Every candidate receives clear feedback. If not promoted, they get specific gaps and a plan.

**Principles for fairness**
- Use the same criteria for everyone
- Guard against bias towards visible or vocal contributors; work done quietly counts
- Document decisions and review them for patterns across groups

## 7. Growing in place

Not everyone wants or needs promotion. E3 is a long, valuable career level. Recognise depth of craft, and do not treat promotion as the only measure of progress.
